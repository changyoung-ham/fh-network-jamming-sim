\noindent\textbf{Purpose.}
S1 adds a hopping transmitter and a partial-band noise jammer to the S0 link.
This note derives (i) why hopping buys a processing gain, (ii) the closed-form
BER used to verify the simulator, (iii) the jammer's optimal band fraction
$\rho^\ast$ and the resulting inverse-linear BER law, (iv) the band fraction
that matters for a link \emph{outage}, which S2 builds on, and (v) the jamming
margin numbers reported in \texttt{results/s1\_link\_budget.txt}.
The worst-case analysis in Section~3 is the standard textbook treatment of
pulsed and partial-band noise jamming \cite{sklar,simon}; it is reproduced here
so that every number in the code can be traced to an equation.
Mathematics used: total probability, one-variable optimisation.

\section{Processing gain}

The data occupies bandwidth $W_d$; the transmitter hops among $N_{\mathrm{ch}}$
channels spread over $W_{ss}\approx N_{\mathrm{ch}}W_d$. A jammer with total
power $J$ that wants to cover the whole hopping band must spread $J$ over
$W_{ss}$, so its density is $N_J = J/W_{ss}$. The transmitter's signal at any
instant occupies only $W_d$, where the jammer delivers only
$N_J W_d = J\,W_d/W_{ss} = J/N_{\mathrm{ch}}$ of its power. The jammer
effectively loses a factor
\[
  G_p = \frac{W_{ss}}{W_d} \approx N_{\mathrm{ch}}, \qquad
  G_p\,[\mathrm{dB}] = 10\log_{10}N_{\mathrm{ch}}.
\]
With $N_{\mathrm{ch}}=100$: $G_p = 20$\,dB.

\paragraph{Relating $\EbNJ$ to $J/S$.}
With signal power $S$ and bit rate $R_b$, $E_b = S/R_b$. Taking $W_d\approx R_b$
(a different bandwidth convention changes $G_p$ by a constant and shifts every
$J/S$ axis by the same amount),
\[
  \frac{E_b}{N_J} = \frac{S/R_b}{J/W_{ss}} = \frac{S}{J}\cdot\frac{W_{ss}}{R_b}
  \approx \frac{S}{J}\,G_p
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \boxed{\ \frac{E_b}{N_J}\,[\mathrm{dB}] = G_p\,[\mathrm{dB}] - \frac{J}{S}\,[\mathrm{dB}]\ }.
\]
This is the line \texttt{ebnj\_lin = db\_to\_linear(gp\_db - js\_db)} in the code and
the reason the figure's $x$-axis is $J/S$ while the analysis runs on $\EbNJ$.

\section{Partial-band jamming: the mixture formula}

A jammer need not spread over the whole band. It can concentrate $J$
on a fraction $\rho\in(0,1]$ of the channels, raising its density there to
$N_J/\rho$ and leaving the rest clean. The jammer does not know the
pseudo-random hopping pattern, so each hop lands in the jammed
region independently with probability $\rho$. By the law of total probability,
\begin{equation}
  P_b(\rho) = (1-\rho)\,\Q\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{2E_b}{N_0}}\right)
            + \rho\,\Q\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{2E_b}{N_0 + N_J/\rho}}\right).
  \label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
The first term is the thermal-noise-only BER of S0 (clean hop); the second is an
S0 link whose noise density is $N_0$ plus the concentrated jammer density.
This is \texttt{ber\_partial\_band\_theory()} and it is what the Monte Carlo
(random jammed flag per hop, then the S0 signal model) is checked against,
point by point, with the $z$-score test of the S0 note (\texttt{VERIFICATION 1}).

\paragraph{Hop rate.}
The simulation sends one bit per hop so that successive bits are jammed
independently. Eq.~\eqref{eq:mix} is an \emph{average} over hops and holds for
any number of bits per hop; what changes with slow hopping is the burstiness
of the errors, which matters for coding and is outside this model.

\paragraph{Limit check ($\rho=1$).}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mix} reduces to $\Q\!\big(\sqrt{2E_b/(N_0+N_J)}\big)$: plain AWGN with
the two densities added (\texttt{VERIFICATION 2}, agreement to $10^{-12}$).

\section{The jammer's optimal $\rho$ and the inverse-linear law}

From the jammer's point of view $\rho$ is a free parameter and it wants to
maximise \eqref{eq:mix}. When the jammer dominates the thermal noise
($N_J/\rho \gg N_0$), drop $N_0$:
\[
  P_b(\rho) \approx \rho\,\Q\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{2\rho E_b}{N_J}}\right).
\]
Substitute $x = 2\rho\,\EbNJ$ (so $\rho = x/(2\EbNJ)$):
\[
  P_b \approx \frac{x\,\Q(\sqrt{x})}{2\,\EbNJ} = \frac{g(x)}{2\,\EbNJ},
  \qquad g(x) := x\,\Q(\sqrt x).
\]
The dependence on $\EbNJ$ has been pulled out; the jammer just maximises
$g(x)$ over $x>0$. Setting $g'(x)=0$, with $\Q'(u) = -\varphi(u)$ and
$\varphi(u)=e^{-u^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$,
\[
  g'(x) = \Q(\sqrt x) - x\,\varphi(\sqrt x)\,\frac{1}{2\sqrt x}
        = \Q(\sqrt x) - \frac{\sqrt x}{2}\,\varphi(\sqrt x) = 0.
\]
This transcendental equation has one positive root, found numerically:
\[
  x^\ast \approx 1.418, \qquad g(x^\ast)\approx 0.1657, \qquad \Q(\sqrt{x^\ast}) \approx 0.1169 .
\]
\textbf{Numerical check:} at $x=1.418$ both sides of
$\Q(\sqrt x)=\tfrac{\sqrt x}{2}\varphi(\sqrt x)$ equal $0.1169$.

Two consequences follow.
\begin{align}
  \rho^\ast &= \frac{x^\ast}{2\,\EbNJ} \approx \frac{0.709}{\EbNJ}, \label{eq:rhostar}\\[2pt]
  P_b^{\text{worst}} &= \frac{g(x^\ast)}{2\,\EbNJ} \approx \frac{0.083}{\EbNJ}. \label{eq:worst}
\end{align}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:worst} is the main result of S1: \emph{against an optimal
partial-band jammer the BER decays only as $1/(\EbNJ)$}, not exponentially as
in S0. (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhostar} is only meaningful while $\rho^\ast\le 1$, i.e.\
$\EbNJ \ge x^\ast/2 \approx -1.5$\,dB; below that the jammer uses the
whole band.) These constants are the ones quoted for coherent BPSK in
\cite{sklar}. For noncoherent BFSK, the modulation more commonly paired with
frequency hopping, the same argument gives $\rho^\ast = 2/(\EbNJ)$ and
$P_b^{\text{worst}} = e^{-1}/(\EbNJ)$ \cite{sklar,simon}: a different constant,
the same inverse-linear law. Coherent BPSK is used here only because it
continues the S0 model.

\paragraph{Check of the optimiser.}
The script finds $\rho^\ast$ with a bounded one-dimensional search on the exact
formula \eqref{eq:mix}. With thermal noise switched off it must return the
constants above; \texttt{VERIFICATION 3} obtains
$\rho^\ast\,\EbNJ = 0.7088$ and $P_b^{\text{worst}}\,\EbNJ = 0.08286$.

\paragraph{The number in the figure.}
At $J/S = 12$\,dB with $G_p=20$\,dB, $\EbNJ = 8$\,dB $=6.31$. Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhostar}
gives $\rho^\ast = 0.709/6.31 = 0.112$ with thermal noise ignored. Keeping the
thermal term $N_0$ in \eqref{eq:mix} shifts the optimum upward; the search on
the exact formula gives $\rho^\ast = 0.135$. The curve is flat near its maximum
(the BER at $\rho=0.10$ and at $\rho=0.16$ is within 3\,\% of the peak), so the
Monte Carlo points are used to confirm the \emph{curve}, not to locate the
maximum. The worst-case BER is
$1.42\times10^{-2}$ versus $2.70\times10^{-3}$ for a full-band jammer of the same
power, a factor of $5.2$.

\section{The band fraction that causes an outage}

S2 declares a link \emph{down} when $P_b > \mathrm{BER}_{\mathrm{out}}$. The
jammer then asks a different question: not ``which $\rho$ maximises the BER of
this link'' but ``what is the strongest link I can still bring down''. By
Eq.~\eqref{eq:worst} the worst-case BER reaches $\mathrm{BER}_{\mathrm{out}}$ when
\begin{equation}
  \left(\EbNJ\right)_{\mathrm{out}} = \frac{0.0829}{\mathrm{BER}_{\mathrm{out}}},
  \qquad
  \rho_{\mathrm{out}} = \frac{0.709}{(\EbNJ)_{\mathrm{out}}}
  = \frac{\mathrm{BER}_{\mathrm{out}}}{\Q(\sqrt{x^\ast})} \approx 8.55\,\mathrm{BER}_{\mathrm{out}} .
  \label{eq:rhoout}
\end{equation}
For $\mathrm{BER}_{\mathrm{out}}=10^{-3}$: $(\EbNJ)_{\mathrm{out}} = 82.9 = 19.2$\,dB and
$\rho_{\mathrm{out}} = 0.0086$. This is Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhostar} evaluated at the
outage point, not a new optimisation; it is much smaller than the $0.135$ of
the figure only because $19.2$\,dB is a much larger $\EbNJ$ than $8$\,dB.

\paragraph{One-channel limit.}
A jammer facing $N_{\mathrm{ch}}$ channels cannot jam less than one of them, so
$\rho\ge 1/N_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0.01$. Here $\rho_{\mathrm{out}}=0.0086$ is just below
that limit. The script optimises $\rho$ as a continuous variable, which keeps
the link to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:rhostar}--\eqref{eq:worst}, and reports the
one-channel case alongside; the two differ by $0.01$\,dB (next section).

\section{Jamming margin}

A link is \emph{up} if $P_b \le \mathrm{BER}_{\mathrm{out}} = 10^{-3}$. The value is
an assumption; the S2 note shows that changing it shifts every threshold by
10\,dB per decade and leaves the dependence on network size unchanged.
Solve the BER formula for the $\EbNJ$ at which the criterion is just met,
$(\EbNJ)_{\mathrm{req}}$, then allow an implementation loss $L_{\mathrm{sys}}$
(assumed 2\,dB; it shifts all margins equally). The jamming margin is the $J/S$
the link can absorb:
\[
  M_J\,[\mathrm{dB}] = G_p - (\EbNJ)_{\mathrm{req}} - L_{\mathrm{sys}}.
\]
For $N_{\mathrm{ch}}=100$, $\EbNo=10$\,dB, $L_{\mathrm{sys}}=2$\,dB:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
jammer strategy & optimum $\rho$ & $(\EbNJ)_{\mathrm{req}}$ & $M_J$ \\
\midrule
full band, $\rho=1$ & --- & 9.61\,dB & $\mathbf{8.39}$\,dB \\
optimal $\rho$ (continuous) & 0.0095 & 19.54\,dB & $\mathbf{-1.54}$\,dB \\
optimal $\rho\ge1/N_{\mathrm{ch}}$ & 0.0100 & 19.53\,dB & $\mathbf{-1.53}$\,dB \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The required $19.54$\,dB is slightly above the $19.2$\,dB of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhoout} because the thermal noise ($\EbNo=10$\,dB) is not
negligible inside the jammed channels.
The optimal jammer needs 9.9\,dB \emph{less} power than a full-band jammer to
break the link. The negative margin means that, at
$\mathrm{BER}_{\mathrm{out}}=10^{-3}$ and without coding, 100 hop channels do
not protect a BPSK link against a jammer of equal received power.
